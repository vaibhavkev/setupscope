Intonation check: where should each bridge saddle go?

For each string: play the 12th-fret harmonic (touch the string lightly above the 12th fret), then fret the same string at the 12th fret and play. If the fretted note is sharp, the saddle must move back; if flat, forward. Tune the open string first.

Method and limits

  • Pitch: McLeod normalised square-difference method on the last ~85 ms of audio, then refined to the fundamental with two band-pass filters centred on that estimate (this removes a ~4-cent sharp bias from stretched overtones just after the pluck). Raw microphone, with echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto-gain turned off.
  • Overtones: 32,768-point spectrum (≈1.5 Hz bins) with peak interpolation; inharmonicity B fitted from fn = n·f1·√((1+Bn²)/(1+B)).
  • Saddle shift: moving the saddle back by Δ lowers the 12th-fret note relative to the harmonic by Δ/L, so Δ = L(k−1)/(2−k) with k = 2error/1200. About 0.37 mm per cent on a 648 mm scale.
  • Tension uses published unit weights (D'Addario EXL110 and EXL120 sets); other strings need the maker's figure (Custom).
  • Limits: noisy rooms, several strings ringing at once, and very weak harmonics reduce accuracy. Phone microphones roll off below ~80 Hz; the low E still works because its overtones carry the pattern.
