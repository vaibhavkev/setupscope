Is your guitar in tune higher up the neck?

Open strings can be perfectly in tune while chords up the neck sound sour. That is set by where each string rests on the bridge (the saddle). This check measures it and tells you how far to move each saddle.

Choose a string (tune it first):
  1. Touch the string lightly right above the 12th fret and pluck: a bell-like note. Let it ring.
  2. Mute it. Now press the string down at the 12th fret and pluck.

What this app measures

  • Tuning: the pitch of each string from the microphone, shown as cents off target (100 cents = one semitone). In tune = within ±3 cents.
  • Steadiness: how the pitch moves over 10 seconds, to spot a slipping peg or a new string still stretching.
  • Intonation: the 12th-fret fretted note against the 12th-fret harmonic, converted into a saddle move in millimetres.
  • Tuning change: for alternate tunings, how much slacker each string becomes, from its tension T = 4·μ·L²·f² (μ = string mass per metre from the maker's data).

Can you trust the readings?

The self-test makes plucked-string sounds at known pitches (with realistic overtones, decay and noise) and runs them through the same detector the tuner uses.

You can also check against another tuner: play a reference tone from this phone into it, or from a laptop into this app.

How it works and its limits

  • Pitch: McLeod normalised square-difference method on ~85 ms of raw microphone audio (echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto-gain off), refined to the fundamental with two band-pass filters. Updates every screen frame.
  • Auto mode picks the nearest string in the chosen tuning. If a string is more than about 2.5 semitones off, pick it by hand.
  • Saddle move: Δ = L(k−1)/(2−k), k = 2error/1200, L = scale length; about 0.37 mm per cent on a 648 mm scale.
  • Tension uses D'Addario's published unit weights for 10–46 and 9–42 sets; other strings can be entered in Settings.
  • Works best with one string ringing in a quiet room. Phone microphones are weak below ~80 Hz; the low E still works through its overtones.

Settings

Scale length is the distance from the nut to the 12th fret, doubled. Most electric guitars are 25.5" (Fender-style) or 24.75" (Gibson-style).
