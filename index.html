About

Accuracy

The self-test makes plucked-string sounds at known pitches, with realistic overtones, decay and noise, and runs them through the same detector the tuner uses.

What it measures
Tuning. The pitch of each string, as cents off target (100 cents = one semitone). In tune means within ±3 cents.
Intonation. The fretted 12th-fret note against the 12th-fret harmonic, turned into a saddle move: Δ = L(k−1)/(2−k), k = 2cents/1200, about 0.37 mm per cent on a 648 mm scale.
Tuning changes. String tension T = 4·μ·L²·f² from the maker's mass per metre μ, compared with standard tuning.
How it works
Pitch is found with the McLeod normalised square-difference method on about 85 ms of raw microphone audio, then refined to the fundamental with two band-pass filters. It updates every screen frame.
Works best with one string ringing in a quiet room. If a string is more than about two semitones off, choose it by hand. Tension uses D'Addario's published unit weights.
